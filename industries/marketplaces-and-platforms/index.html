Messaging for marketplaces and platforms

A marketplace messages two audiences about the same event for opposite reasons: the buyer wants it to arrive, the seller wants proof that it did. That makes the receipt the part worth getting right.

Open the consoleAll industries

Two audiences, one event

  • Order placed, confirmed and shipped — to both sides
  • Dispute, refund and chargeback status changes
  • Payout and settlement notices to sellers
  • Review and rating prompts after completion
  • Account and verification notices

The receipt is the audit trail

When a seller disputes whether they were told something, the answer has to be a record rather than a recollection. The platform logs each send, normalizes provider delivery receipts into one status and can push those events to your own endpoint — so the record lives in your system too.

Where your platform ends and ours starts

Your platform decides who is notified and what the notification says. On the SMS channel the messaging layer decides how it leaves the building — which connected account carries it, at what pace, and what receipt comes back.

Questions this usually raises

Can we tell whether a specific seller was notified?
Sends and their statuses are logged, and receipts can be pushed to an endpoint you control. Note that a receipt means the destination network accepted the message, not that it was read.
Is there a callback retry policy?
Callback delivery is deliberately not treated as fire-and-forget; the reliability model and its limits are set out in the callback guide.
Can different sellers use their own provider accounts?
Connections are held per tenant. Whether each seller is a separate tenant, and therefore has their own connections and API keys, is a design decision on your side.
